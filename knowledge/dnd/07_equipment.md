# Equipment

## Weapons

Weapons split first into **simple** (easy to use effectively with minimal training — clubs, daggers,
handaxes, light crossbows, spears, quarterstaffs) and **martial** (require dedicated training —
longswords, battleaxes, longbows, rapiers, warhammers, and similar), and then into **melee** vs
**ranged**. Many weapons carry special properties:

- **Finesse** — can use Dexterity instead of Strength for attack and damage (rapiers, daggers, shortswords).
- **Light** — small enough to support two-weapon fighting.
- **Heavy** — awkward for Small creatures, who take disadvantage using them.
- **Two-Handed** — requires both hands, usually trading versatility for higher damage.
- **Versatile** — usable one- or two-handed, with a higher damage die two-handed.
- **Reach** — extends melee range by 5 feet (spears, glaives, halberds).
- **Thrown** — can be used as a ranged weapon even if primarily melee (handaxes, javelins, daggers).

## Armor

| Category | AC formula (typical) | Stealth | Strength requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light armor (padded, leather, studded leather) | 11–12 + full DEX modifier | No penalty | None |
| Medium armor (hide, chain shirt, breastplate, half plate) | 12–15 + DEX modifier (max +2) | Usually no penalty | None |
| Heavy armor (ring mail, chain mail, splint, plate) | 14–18 flat, no DEX modifier | Disadvantage on Stealth | Often a minimum Strength score |
| Shield | +2 AC, held in one hand | — | None |

Wearing armor without proficiency in that category typically imposes disadvantage on Strength and
Dexterity checks, saves, and attack rolls, plus prevents spellcasting — proficiency isn't just flavor,
it's mechanically required to use heavier armor effectively.

## Adventuring gear

Beyond weapons and armor, a typical starting kit includes: a backpack, bedroll, rope (50 ft, hempen or
silk), rations, a waterskin, a tinderbox, torches or lantern with oil, a healer's kit, and
class-appropriate tools (thieves' tools for a rogue, a component pouch or arcane focus for a caster,
an instrument for a bard). Currency is typically tracked in copper, silver, electrum, gold, and
platinum pieces, at a standard 10-to-1 conversion between adjacent tiers (10 copper = 1 silver, and
so on), letting parties price everything from a loaf of bread to a suit of plate armor on one scale.
