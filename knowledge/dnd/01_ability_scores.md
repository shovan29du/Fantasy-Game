# Ability Scores

D&D 5th-edition-style characters are defined by six core ability scores, each ranging from 1 to 30 for
most creatures (typical adventurers start between 8 and 18 before racial and other bonuses). Every
score has a **modifier**, calculated as `floor((score - 10) / 2)`, which is the number actually added
to most dice rolls that depend on that ability.

| Score | Governs | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength (STR)** | Physical power | Melee attack/damage rolls, carrying capacity, athletics |
| **Dexterity (DEX)** | Agility and reflexes | Ranged attacks, Armor Class, initiative, stealth, acrobatics |
| **Constitution (CON)** | Endurance and health | Hit points, concentration saves, resisting poison/disease |
| **Intelligence (INT)** | Reasoning and memory | Arcane spellcasting (wizards), investigation, lore checks |
| **Wisdom (WIS)** | Awareness and intuition | Divine/nature spellcasting (clerics, druids), perception, insight |
| **Charisma (CHA)** | Force of personality | Persuasion, deception, intimidation, some spellcasting (bards, sorcerers, warlocks) |

## Ability checks, saving throws, and proficiency

Most actions that aren't a straightforward attack are resolved as an **ability check**: roll a d20, add
the relevant ability modifier, and compare the total to a Difficulty Class (DC) set by the situation —
easy tasks might be DC 10, dangerous or exacting ones DC 20 or higher.

A **saving throw** uses the same math but represents resisting an effect (a trap, a spell, a poison)
rather than initiating an action.

**Proficiency bonus** is added on top of the ability modifier whenever a character is specifically
trained in the skill, save, or weapon being used. It starts at +2 for a beginning adventurer and rises
to +6 for the most experienced, roughly in step with character level — representing broad competence
that grows independently of raw ability scores.

## Advantage and disadvantage

Instead of stacking numeric bonuses and penalties for every circumstance, favorable conditions grant
**advantage** (roll two d20s, keep the higher) and unfavorable conditions grant **disadvantage** (roll
two d20s, keep the lower). If a character would somehow have both advantage and disadvantage on the
same roll, they cancel out and a single die is rolled normally.
