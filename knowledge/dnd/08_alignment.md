# Alignment

Alignment is a two-axis shorthand for a character's general moral and ethical outlook — useful as a
quick reference for how an NPC or character tends to behave, though most well-written characters break
their alignment's stereotype at least as often as they embody it.

## The ethical axis: Lawful, Neutral, Chaotic
- **Lawful** — values honesty, tradition, and predictable order; tends to keep promises and respect
  hierarchy or codes of conduct, even personally costly ones.
- **Neutral** — decides case by case rather than committing to either extreme; may value personal
  freedom without actively opposing structure, or vice versa.
- **Chaotic** — values personal freedom and spontaneity over convention; distrusts imposed order and
  follows their own conscience over external rules.

## The moral axis: Good, Neutral, Evil
- **Good** — protects others, values altruism, respects the dignity and rights of others.
- **Neutral** — acts in self-interest without malice, prioritizing personal survival or goals over
  either helping or harming others as a matter of principle.
- **Evil** — willing to harm, exploit, or oppress others for personal gain or out of genuine malice.

## The nine alignments

| | Lawful | Neutral | Chaotic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Good** | Lawful Good — the disciplined crusader or knight who works within (or reforms) a system to do right | Neutral Good — does the most good possible without being bound to any particular code | Chaotic Good — a freedom-fighter or rebel who does right regardless of what rules say |
| **Neutral** | Lawful Neutral — values order and law itself, sometimes above any particular moral outcome | True Neutral — balance-seeking, self-interested, or simply indifferent to the whole axis | Chaotic Neutral — a free agent who follows impulse and personal freedom above all else |
| **Evil** | Lawful Evil — a tyrant or strict villain who uses order and rules as tools of domination | Neutral Evil — pursues self-interest without any loyalty to law or chaos, purely pragmatic about harm | Chaotic Evil — destructive and unpredictable, harming others with no regard for law or restraint |

## Using alignment in play

Alignment works best as a starting compass, not a cage: a Lawful Good paladin who breaks an unjust law
to save an innocent is still recognizably lawful and good in the ways that matter, and a character's
alignment can shift meaningfully over a long story as their experiences and relationships change who
they are. Treat it as a description of tendencies, not a rulebook dictating every choice.
