# Classes

A character's class is their trained profession and the primary engine of their mechanical abilities —
determining hit points per level (via a hit die), which saving throws they're naturally proficient in,
and their overall role in a party. The twelve classes below are the traditional open-content core.

| Class | Hit Die | Primary Ability | Save Proficiencies | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barbarian | d12 | Strength | STR, CON | Front-line damage tank; enters a Rage for bonus damage and resistance |
| Bard | d8 | Charisma | DEX, CHA | Versatile support via magic and Bardic Inspiration; jack-of-all-trades |
| Cleric | d8 | Wisdom | WIS, CHA | Divine spellcaster; healing, buffs, and domain-themed combat magic |
| Druid | d8 | Wisdom | INT, WIS | Nature spellcaster; can Wild Shape into animal forms |
| Fighter | d10 | Strength or Dexterity | STR, CON | Most versatile weapon/armor user; extra attacks as they level |
| Monk | d8 | Dexterity & Wisdom | STR, DEX | Unarmed/martial-arts specialist fueled by ki points |
| Paladin | d10 | Strength & Charisma | WIS, CHA | Holy warrior; combines melee combat with divine smites and auras |
| Ranger | d10 | Dexterity & Wisdom | STR, DEX | Wilderness warrior; tracking, favored enemies/terrain, some spellcasting |
| Rogue | d8 | Dexterity | STR, DEX | Stealth and precision; Sneak Attack, skill expertise, evasion |
| Sorcerer | d6 | Charisma | CON, CHA | Innate spellcaster; smaller spell list but flexible Metamagic |
| Warlock | d8 | Charisma | WIS, CHA | Spellcaster empowered by a pact with a powerful patron entity |
| Wizard | d6 | Intelligence | INT, WIS | Broadest spell list of any class, learned through study and a spellbook |

## Hit points and the hit die

At each new level, a character gains hit points equal to a roll of their class's hit die (or its fixed
average, commonly used to avoid the swing of rolling) plus their Constitution modifier. A d12-hit-die
barbarian is built to survive punishment; a d6-hit-die wizard is built to avoid taking hits at all,
relying on spells and positioning instead of raw durability.

## Multiclassing and subclasses

Most systems let a character take levels in more than one class over a career (multiclassing),
blending two roles at the cost of slower progression in either. Within a single class, most characters
also pick a **subclass** — a specialization chosen early (often around level 2–3) that meaningfully
diverges their abilities from other members of the same base class: a Necromancer wizard plays very
differently from an Evoker wizard, despite sharing a hit die and spell list.

## Skill and equipment proficiencies

Each class grants starting proficiency in a handful of skills (chosen from a class-specific list),
certain weapon categories, and sometimes armor — a rogue starts skilled in stealth and deception-leaning
skills with light armor and finesse weapons, while a fighter starts proficient with heavy armor and
nearly every weapon category, reflecting their broader martial training.
