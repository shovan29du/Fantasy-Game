# Combat Basics

Combat is resolved in a sequence of **rounds**, each representing about six seconds of in-fiction time,
during which every combatant gets one turn.

## Initiative

At the start of combat, everyone rolls a d20 plus their Dexterity modifier; the results set turn order
for the entire encounter, highest first. Ties are typically broken by comparing Dexterity scores
directly, or by DM ruling.

## Actions on a turn

On each turn a combatant typically gets:
- **One action** — attack, cast a spell (most spells), Dash (double movement), Disengage (avoid
  opportunity attacks while moving away), Dodge (impose disadvantage on attacks against you), Help,
  Hide, Ready (prepare an action to trigger on a condition), or Search.
- **One bonus action** — only available if a class feature, spell, or item specifically grants one
  (e.g., a rogue's Cunning Action, a two-weapon-fighting off-hand attack).
- **Movement** up to their speed, which can be split before and after their action.
- **One reaction**, usable on someone else's turn when a trigger condition is met (most commonly an
  opportunity attack when an enemy moves out of reach without disengaging).

## Attack rolls

To hit a target, roll a d20 and add the attacker's relevant ability modifier (Strength for most melee,
Dexterity for ranged and finesse weapons) plus proficiency bonus if trained with the weapon. If the
total meets or beats the target's Armor Class (AC), the attack hits. A natural 20 is always a hit (and
typically a **critical hit**, doubling the damage dice rolled); a natural 1 is always a miss.

## Armor Class (AC)

AC represents how hard a creature is to hit — a combination of base armor value, Dexterity modifier
(reduced or capped by heavier armor types), and any shield or magical bonus. Light armor lets a
character use their full Dexterity modifier; heavy armor typically ignores Dexterity entirely in
exchange for a high flat value.

## Damage and death

Damage is subtracted directly from current hit points. At 0 hit points, a character falls unconscious
and must make **death saving throws** — a flat d20 roll each turn on their turn (no modifiers), 10 or
higher counts as a success. Three successes stabilizes them; three failures (or a critical failure/take
enough damage at 0 HP) means death. A natural 20 on a death save immediately restores 1 hit point
instead.

## Cover and range

Partial cover grants +2 AC, three-quarters cover +5 AC, and full cover generally makes a target
untargetable entirely. Ranged weapons have a normal and long range; attacking beyond normal range (but
within long range) imposes disadvantage.
