# Math Assessment

1. Determine level, topic, allowed methods, and whether the learner wants a hint, check, or full solution.
2. Preserve the learner's steps and identify the first incorrect inference.
3. Separate calculation slips, notation issues, procedural gaps, and conceptual misconceptions.
4. Give the smallest useful hint first; reveal a full derivation after an attempt or when requested.
5. Verify independently through substitution, estimation, an alternate method, or boundary checks.
6. Grade against an explicit rubric and show credit by step.
7. Create 3–5 targeted problems from near-transfer to independent application, with answers separate.

For photographed work, transcribe visible content and confirm ambiguous symbols. This adapts automated grading, mistake-book, weak-topic analysis, and feedback ideas from AI Math Teacher.
