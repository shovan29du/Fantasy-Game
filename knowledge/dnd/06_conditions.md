# Conditions

Conditions are standardized status effects that alter what a creature can do, letting a scene reference
"grappled" or "poisoned" without re-explaining the mechanics every time.

| Condition | Effect summary |
|---|---|
| **Blinded** | Can't see; automatically fails sight-based checks; attacks against it have advantage, its attacks have disadvantage |
| **Charmed** | Can't attack the charmer or target them with harmful effects; the charmer has advantage on social checks against them |
| **Deafened** | Can't hear; automatically fails hearing-based checks |
| **Exhaustion** | Tracked in levels (1–6 in most versions); each level adds cumulative penalties (disadvantage on checks, speed reduction, and eventually death at maximum) |
| **Frightened** | Disadvantage on checks/attacks while the source of fear is in sight; can't willingly move closer to it |
| **Grappled** | Speed becomes 0; ends if the grappler is incapacitated or the target is forcibly moved out of reach |
| **Incapacitated** | Can't take actions or reactions |
| **Invisible** | Can't be seen without special senses; attacks against it have disadvantage, its attacks have advantage |
| **Paralyzed** | Incapacitated, can't move or speak; automatically fails STR/DEX saves; attacks against it have advantage and automatically crit if the attacker is within 5 feet |
| **Petrified** | Transformed to stone; incapacitated, unaware, resistant to all damage, immune to poison and disease |
| **Poisoned** | Disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks |
| **Prone** | Can only crawl unless it stands up; attacks against it have advantage if the attacker is within 5 feet (otherwise disadvantage); its own attacks have disadvantage |
| **Restrained** | Speed 0; disadvantage on Dexterity saves; attacks against it have advantage, its attacks have disadvantage |
| **Stunned** | Incapacitated, can't move, can speak only falteringly; automatically fails STR/DEX saves; attacks against it have advantage |
| **Unconscious** | Incapacitated, can't move or speak, unaware of surroundings, drops what it's holding and falls prone; attacks against it have advantage and automatically crit within 5 feet |

## Why conditions matter for roleplay

Conditions give a shorthand for consequences that persist scene to scene — a character knocked prone in
one exchange is still prone (and easier to hit) until they spend part of a future turn standing back
up; a grappled character genuinely can't simply walk away until the grapple is broken. Tracking them
consistently is part of what keeps combat and tense scenes feeling like they have real stakes rather
than resetting to neutral every exchange.
